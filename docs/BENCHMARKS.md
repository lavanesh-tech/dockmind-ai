# Benchmark methodology

The numbers are in **[benchmarks/RESULTS.md](benchmarks/RESULTS.md)** (with
[results.csv](benchmarks/results.csv) and [summary.json](benchmarks/summary.json)). Those files
are generated by `BenchmarkRunner` on every `./mvnw verify` and committed with the code that
produced them. They are never edited by hand.

## What is compared

| | FCFS (baseline) | PRIORITY_AWARE (design iteration) | DOCKMIND_DISPATCH (DockMind) |
|---|---|---|---|
| How it works | Book trucks in arrival order | Book trucks in urgency order across the whole day | Move a clock through the day; when a dock and crew are free, start the most urgent truck **already waiting** |
| Who goes first | Arrival order | Priority → tightest deadline → arrival | Same urgency order, but only among arrived trucks |
| Which dock | Earliest start | Earliest start + 20 min per unneeded special capability | Free dock with fewest unneeded special capabilities |
| Feasibility rules | Shared `Timeline`: capabilities, arrival, deadline, open hours, outages, crew capacity | identical | identical |

All three use the same feasibility code, so differences come only from the decision rules.

**Why three?** PRIORITY_AWARE was the first design. Benchmarking showed it protects urgent
trucks but makes standard trucks wait longer, because booking urgent trucks across the whole
day first fragments the timeline. The dispatcher fixes that by never reserving a slot for a
truck that hasn't arrived. Both are kept so the improvement is visible and reproducible.

**Reading the numbers:** a lower average wait is only better if the same trucks were served.
Under heavy load, FCFS leaves more trucks unscheduled, most of them urgent trucks that missed
their deadlines, and those trucks aren't in its average. Compare the *Unscheduled* columns
before the *wait* columns.
The baseline isn't weakened. It uses every dock and fills gaps, which is roughly what a careful
dispatcher does by hand without priorities.

## Workload model (synthetic assumptions, not measured data)

- 8 docks, open 06:00–22:00: 2 refrigerated, 1 hazmat, 1 side-load + oversized, 4 general.
- 6 loading crews: at most 6 docks loading at the same time.
- Arrivals: a two-peak day (around 09:00 and 14:00, σ = 2 h), clamped to 06:00–19:30.
- Needs: 20% refrigerated, 5% hazmat, 5% side-load, 3% oversized, 67% none.
- Priority: 80% standard (4 h deadline), 15% expedited (90 min), 5% critical (30 min).
- Loading time: 10 min + 0.75 min per pallet, 1–60 pallets (about 33 min on average).
- Loads of 100, 140 and 180 trucks per day (about 55%, 80% and 100% of crew capacity), 5 seeds each (1–5).
  An earlier calibration (15 min + 1.5 min/pallet at 150–350 trucks) was about 150% overloaded,
  so most results measured an impossible day. It was replaced.

## Metrics

- **Wait** = loading start − truck arrival, for scheduled trucks. Unscheduled trucks are counted
  separately; averaging them in as zero would flatter the result.
- **p95 wait:** nearest-rank 95th percentile.
- **Urgent:** expedited and critical trucks only.
- **Dock utilization** = busy dock time ÷ (open time − outages), over all docks.
- **Compute time:** median of 5 timed runs after 2 warm-up runs on the build machine. It varies
  between machines and runs.
- **Violations:** every schedule is checked by the independent `ScheduleValidator`. The runner
  refuses to write results if any schedule has a violation.

## Honest limitations

- These are simulations of a model the project defined. They are **not** measurements from a
  real warehouse and say nothing certain about real-world savings.
- The schedulers are heuristics, not optimal solvers.
- Under heavy load the dispatcher's *average* wait can be higher than FCFS's, because it serves
  trucks FCFS drops. Urgent trucks wait far less and fewer trucks go unserved. The table shows both.
- Disruption recovery (M5a) is in the "Disruption recovery" section of `RESULTS.md` and
  `recovery.csv`: 140 trucks, decision at 12:00, seeded late arrivals and two-hour dock outages,
  comparing how many appointments each strategy moves and whether every plan is valid.
- API latency under load: `./scripts/run-local.sh load` writes `docs/benchmarks/load/RESULTS.md` (p50/p95/p99,
  failures) from a real run on the developer's machine. Outbox throughput and recovery planning time
  are live metrics on the Grafana dashboard.
